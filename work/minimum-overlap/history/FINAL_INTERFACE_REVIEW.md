# Mathematical interface review

This records the campaign's own analytic review, not external peer review
or formal verification. Actual execution receipts remain separate evidence.

The density and its interval complement are zero outside [0,2]. Their
masses are one. Consequently integral p=1, and the first overlap moment
m=2-2 integral x*h(x) is in [-1,1]. The endpoint range uses the box and
mass constraints, not just support. Reflection changes the sign of m.

The lower claim is uniform over arbitrary measurable densities. Every
member of a finite closed mean cover has a rational strict margin above
the selected target. Taking their finite minimum gives a uniform strict
margin, which is needed before taking the infimum over densities.
Pointwise strict inequalities without this uniform margin would not suffice.

For the physical odd profile, d(t)=H(t)+H(2-t)-1 on [0,2], so it is
1-Lipschitz and satisfies d(2-t)=d(t). The even profile F need not be
Lipschitz for a merely measurable h. No finite-cell estimate for F may
silently borrow the Lipschitz bound for d.

In the capacity return, c(t)=|Q(t)|+|Q(2-t)| satisfies
integral_0^1 |c'|^2 <= integral_-2^2 |Q'|^2 on the aligned smooth cells.
The factor two from squaring the derivative sum is paid by the even full
kernel energy. Cauchy-Schwarz and sum h_i^5<=h_max^4 yield the stated
covariance error h_max^2*sqrt(E_Q/120). Every kernel jump is a cell
boundary. The triangle-integral lower coefficient and every flow residual
are conservative; numerical LP feasibility is not a proof assumption.

The radius return reuses a fixed positive-part denominator and a verified
quadratic numerator. It does not relabel the actual executed target or
radius. The old job named 627424 selected global .380627423, while its
central checks passed .380627424 at .00007. These are different facts.

The upper witness is a step density on a nonuniform grid. Interval-pair
overlap lengths are affine between all differences of endpoints. Exact
checks of all 86017 shift knots therefore cover every intermediate shift.
The probabilistic discrete return handles zero shift and cardinality
balancing separately; the continuous fractional overlap at zero is not
mistaken for the discrete zero-shift difference count.

The dyadic and directed subadditivity returns hold for every measurable
density by a pointwise scalar inequality and domain enlargement with
nonnegative integrands. Their exact hat identities do not require a step
density. They are separately typed premises. A mean-zero kernel bound,
an excluded virtual profile or a negative fixed-line derivative is not a
global bound on mu. Sampled root lists are not treated as exhaustive.
